Module 5 Assignment: Unsupervised Learning with College Data

Introduction

In this assignment you will apply Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and clustering methods to the Colleges 2023 dataset. Rather than predicting graduation rates, we will use unsupervised methods to explore the structure of the data and group similar institutions together.

We will work with a stratified sample1 of 70 colleges including University of the Pacific, some peer institutions like Santa Clara, and some top schools like Harvard. Use the following code to load and prepare your sample:

library(tidyverse)
set.seed(133)

colleges <- read_csv("https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jmayberr/math133-stat-learning-fall2026/main/Datasets/colleges2023.csv")

must_include <- c("University of the Pacific",
                  "Stanford University",
                  "University of California-Berkeley",
                  "Harvard University",
                  "University of Southern California",
                  "University of California-Los Angeles",
                  "University of San Diego",
                  "Gonzaga University",
                  "Santa Clara University",
                  "Massachusetts Institute of Technology")

colleges_sample <- bind_rows(
  # Include 10 colleges listed above
  colleges |> 
    filter(inst_name %in% must_include),
  # Randomly sample 60 other colleges with 20 each from low, mid, and high grad rate groups
  colleges |>
    filter(!inst_name %in% must_include) |>
    mutate(grad_rate_group = cut(grad_rate_6yr, breaks = 3,
                                 labels = c("low", "mid", "high"))) |>
    group_by(grad_rate_group) |>
    slice_sample(n = 20) |>
    ungroup() |>
    select(-grad_rate_group)
) |>
  column_to_rownames("inst_name")

Problem 1: PCA — Variance Explained

Perform PCA on the sample dataset (remember to scale your variables). Answer the following:

  • Create a scree plot of the principal components. How many components would you retain based on the elbow rule?
  • Use summary() to report the cumulative proportion of variance explained by your chosen number of components. Does this support your choice from the scree plot?

Problem 2: PCA — Interpreting Components

  • Examine the loadings of the first two principal components from your PCA output. What types of institutions would have a high PC1 score? A high PC2 score? Use specific variable names to support your interpretation.
  • Create a variable plot using fviz_pca_var() or biplot(scale=1). Which variables are most strongly associated with PC1? Which with PC2? Are any variables highly correlated with each other based on the direction of their arrows?
  • Create a biplot showing both variable arrows and institution scores. Identify where University of the Pacific falls in the plot and discuss what its position suggests about its characteristics relative to other institutions.

Problem 3: K-Means Clustering

  • Use the elbow method based on WSS to determine an appropriate number of clusters \(K\).
  • Fit a K-means model with your chosen \(K\) and visualize the clusters using fviz_cluster() or ggplot().
  • Which cluster does University of the Pacific belong to? Name two or three other institutions in the same cluster.

Problem 4: Cluster Characterization

  • Create a heatmap, line, or faceted bar chart of the standardized cluster means. What characterizes each cluster in terms of the original variables?
  • Based on the cluster means, give each cluster a descriptive label (e.g. “high resource selective institutions”) and justify your labels.
  • Does University of the Pacific’s cluster assignment make intuitive sense given its characteristics?

Problem 5: Hierarchical Clustering

  • Fit a hierarchical clustering model using complete linkage on the scaled data. Plot the dendrogram with your chosen number of clusters highlighted.
  • Which institution is University of the Pacific’s closest neighbor in the dendrogram?
  • Compare the cluster assignments from hierarchical clustering to those from K-means. Do the two methods broadly agree? Are there any institutions that end up in different clusters across the two methods?
